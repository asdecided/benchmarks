\section{Method}

\subsection{Arms}
All arms are grounding strategies behind a uniform adapter; none is privileged.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{no\_grounding} --- the task with no decision context (floor).
  \item \texttt{context\_dump} --- every decision artifact pasted into context.
  \item \texttt{naive\_rag} --- embeddings with top-$k$ retrieval over the same
        documents, using asymmetric query/document embeddings.
  \item \texttt{rac} --- a deterministic, typed grounding layer that retrieves by
        decision type and \emph{follows} machine-stated supersession edges,
        replacing a retrieved superseded decision with its live successor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Fairness controls}
Every arm feeds context to the \emph{same} fixed answering model with the
\emph{same} prompt scaffold, pinned by model, version, temperature, and seed.
Each arm receives \emph{one} symmetric opportunity to populate the context window;
arms differ only in selection and assembly. We isolate retrieval/assembly quality:
the benchmark measures, given one symmetric shot at the context window, which
assembly strategy yields better decision adherence. It does \emph{not} test
whether a pull-based grounding tool is invoked at the right moment in
production---a separate deployment question, out of scope.

\subsection{Co-primary outcomes}
\benchname{} reports two co-primary, deterministic outcomes; each claim stands
alone, and the conjunction is claimed only if both hold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Decision adherence} --- structural: did the proposed change
        respect the governing decision? The novel construct, scored by this
        benchmark's own deterministic scorer (below).
  \item \textbf{Decision-conditioned resolution} --- executable: on
        version-conditioned problems adapted from
        GitChameleon~\citep{gitchameleon}, where the correct completion depends
        on a recorded version-pin decision the prompt deliberately never
        restates, does the solution pass the upstream harness's unit tests?
        The upstream tests are the sole scorer; we add none.
\end{itemize}
The honesty rule covers both: a mixed result (adherence improves, resolution
does not, or the reverse) is reported plainly as the finding.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
The analysis is pre-registered (a frozen amendment to the benchmark's
specification) and deterministic. Arms answer the same scenarios under common
random numbers, so comparisons are paired: the unit is the scenario for
base-$N$ runs, the scenario--seed pair for the corpus-size sweep, and the
example for the executable outcome. Per arm pair we report the exact McNemar
test (a two-sided binomial on the discordant pairs; no asymptotic
approximation), the paired risk difference with a Wald 95\% interval, the
conditional odds ratio with a Wilson-transformed interval, and Wilson intervals
on the marginal rates; zero-cell tables are flagged degenerate rather than
continuity-corrected. The confirmatory comparison is \texttt{rac} vs
\texttt{naive\_rag} adherence at the largest corpus size ($N{=}300$,
$\alpha{=}0.05$); intermediate $N$ are secondary with Holm correction, and
$N{=}10$ is descriptive (a pre-registered expected tie). Seed-level $t$-based
intervals on the paired difference are retained as supporting variance
evidence. Every statistic regenerates from the committed run records; none of
them gates anything.

\subsection{Deterministic scoring}
The answering model returns a structured proposed change, scored by structural
inspection rather than a learned judge:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Decision adherence} --- did the change respect the governing
        decision (cite the live decision; not follow a superseded one; not perform
        a prohibited action)?
  \item \textbf{Governing-decision recall} --- did the arm's assembled grounding
        actually \emph{contain} the binding decision? This is the mechanistic
        explanation for adherence.
  \item \textbf{Stale-decision rate}, \textbf{false-permit rate}, and
        \textbf{false-prohibit rate}.
\end{itemize}
There is deliberately no composite score, and no embedding- or LLM-based scoring,
so results are reproducible from the recorded outputs.
