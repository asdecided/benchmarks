\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The falsifier.} \benchname{} pre-registers a falsifier: if the grounded
arm is statistically indistinguishable from \texttt{naive\_rag} on
superseded-decision and prohibition scenarios at $N \geq 50$, the retrieval thesis
is not supported and we report that. A benchmark whose hypothesis cannot fail is
not a measurement. \todo{report the outcome.}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One answering model.} Results are reported for a single pinned
        model; generality across models is future work. \todo{extend to multiple
        models for a stronger claim.}
  \item \textbf{Scope of scenarios.} The reported corpora are standards-style
        decisions (PEP/RFC/W3C); whether the effect transfers to in-repo ADRs and
        product decisions is an open question.
  \item \textbf{Symmetric-injection caveat.} The benchmark measures
        retrieval/assembly quality given one symmetric shot at the context window.
        It does not measure whether a pull-based grounding tool is \emph{invoked}
        at the right time in a deployed agent; reading a favourable result as
        ``this fixes adherence in production'' overstates what was measured.
  \item \textbf{Cost is list-price.} Token costs are priced at published rates and
        do not reflect proxy markups or negotiated contracts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Threats to validity.} Gold labels are authored blind and the taxonomy
and rubric are frozen before results exist, mitigating the risk of fitting the
question to the winner; the negative control guards against an arm that wins by
refusing everything; verbatim, pinned, hashed corpora mitigate cherry-picking.
